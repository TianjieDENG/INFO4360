Curriculum

The arc of the course

Roughly the order the course moves through. Each unit adds a rung to the “ladder of approaches” from the approach page. Dates, readings, and assignments live in Canvas.

  1. Text as data

    From raw documents to a matrix: tokenization, normalization, word frequency, TF-IDF. The cheapest rung of the ladder, and the baseline every heavier method has to beat.

  2. Linguistic features and regular expressions

    Extracting structure from text — parts of speech, named entities, hand-written patterns. Deterministic, transparent, and free to run when a language model would be overkill.

  3. Word and document embeddings

    Representing meaning as vectors: similarity, analogy, and the biases that come along for the ride. Semantic search over a corpus. The bridge between classical methods and everything modern.

  4. Text classification and evaluation

    Supervised models from TF-IDF with logistic regression to embedding-based classifiers. Confusion matrices, precision and recall, cross-validation, class imbalance, and honest error analysis.

  5. Sentiment analysis, three ways

    Lexicon methods, supervised learning, and LLM prompting on the same dataset — compared head to head on accuracy, cost, and latency.

  6. Transformers

    How attention and pretraining work; contextual embeddings; the BERT and GPT families; and when fine-tuning a small model still beats prompting a large one.

  7. Working with language models

    Prompting as engineering, structured extraction from business documents, and retrieval-augmented generation grounded in a private corpus.

  8. LLM-assisted labeling and output evaluation

    Using a language model to annotate data, then distilling it into a cheap classifier. Evaluating open-ended output for hallucination, consistency, and bias, including model-as-judge and its failure modes.

  9. Topic modeling and theme discovery

    LDA as the interpretable baseline, against embedding clustering and LLM-based theme extraction on the same corpus.

  10. Using text to predict

    Combining text-derived features with structured data to model a business outcome. Leakage, interpretation, and what the text is really contributing.

  11. Cost, risk, and deployment

    API versus open-weights versus classical at scale; privacy and governance; prompt injection; a brief look at agentic workflows; and a closing return to the ladder and the “should we even use an LLM?” decision.
